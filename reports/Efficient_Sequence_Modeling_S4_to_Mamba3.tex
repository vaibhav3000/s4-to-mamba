\title{Efficient Sequence Modeling: From S4D to \mamba{}-3\\
\large An Experimental Study of Five Sequence Architectures in Pure PyTorch}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Vaibhav Mahore}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Undergraduate Programme, Mathematics and Computing}\\ \textit{Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bengaluru, India}\\ mvaibhav@iisc.ac.in}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Five sequence backbones, a causal Transformer, S4D, \mamba{}-1, \mamba{}-2
and \mamba{}-3, are implemented from first principles in pure PyTorch behind
one interface that exposes both a parallel form for training and an $O(1)$
recurrent step for generation (a KV cache for the Transformer). For
\mamba{}-3 we derive and implement a chunked parallel form, a complex SSD
with a two-band correction for the exponential-trapezoidal rule, and verify
it against the recurrence in float64 and with \texttt{gradcheck}. Models
are parameter-matched per task and trained with three run seeds under
deterministic algorithms on four synthetic tasks and IMDb; a separate
benchmark measures training steps, time to first token, decode latency,
memory, reduced-precision error and \texttt{torch.compile} readiness on a
6\,GB laptop GPU. \mamba{}-3 has the best mean on cumulative parity
(66.0\,$\pm$\,20.1\% token accuracy against at most 57.7\% for every other
model) but only one of its three seeds learns the task (89.2\%), and no
model extrapolates beyond the training length. Selective models solve
selective copying in distribution (98 to 99\%); out of distribution
\mamba{}-1 and \mamba{}-2 keep about half the answers and \mamba{}-3 falls
to 14\%. On IMDb all models land between 81.6 and 84.5\%. At a 32K prompt
the SSM decode states take 0.14 to 0.39\,MiB against a 64\,MiB bf16 KV
cache. In bf16 the fused attention kernels still give the fastest prefill at
every length; in fp32 the FFT prefill of S4D overtakes attention from 16K.
Every number comes from committed result files.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Self-attention computes interactions between all pairs of positions, so its
cost grows quadratically with sequence length $T$, and a Transformer
generating text keeps a key-value cache that grows linearly with the
context \cite{vaswani2017attention}. State space models (SSMs) replace
attention with a linear recurrence over a fixed-size state: $O(T)$ work over
a sequence and $O(1)$ state per generated token
\cite{gu2021lssl,gu2022efficiently}. S4 and its diagonal variant S4D
\cite{gu2022s4d} are linear time-invariant (LTI) and train as a long
convolution; \mamba{} \cite{gu2023mamba} makes the recurrence
input-dependent; \mamba{}-2 \cite{dao2024transformers} restricts the
transition to a scalar per head so that the recurrence can be computed with
matrix products (state space duality, SSD); and \mamba{}-3
\cite{lahoti2026mamba3} adds complex transitions and an
exponential-trapezoidal discretization.

This study implements all five families in plain PyTorch, without fused
kernels, and asks three questions on one consumer GPU: which capabilities
each architecture shows on small controlled tasks when the comparison is
fair (matched parameters, shared data, several seeds), what the recurrent
state buys at inference compared with a KV cache, and how the
implementations behave under reduced precision and compilation. The
contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
\item One decode interface on every backbone (\texttt{prefill} and
\texttt{step}), new for \mamba{}-2 and \mamba{}-3, and a KV cache for the
Transformer, all tested to reproduce the full forward pass in float64.
\item A chunked parallel form of \mamba{}-3 (Section~\ref{sec:m3}) verified
against its recurrence.
\item Multi-seed results on parity with a rotation ablation and length
extrapolation, selective copying in and out of distribution, exact copying
at a fixed and at variable lengths, and IMDb, with parameter-matched models,
RoPE and sinusoidal Transformers, and pinned attention kernels.
\item Training, prefill and decode measurements from 1K to 32K tokens in
fp32 and bf16, reduced-precision error against float64, and
\texttt{torch.compile} graph-break counts.
\end{itemize}

Scope: S4D rather than full S4; \mamba{}-3 with static rotation
frequencies, real $B$ and $C$ and no MIMO; scans in plain PyTorch. The
results are small-scale and say nothing about fused kernels.

\section{Background: State Space Models}

\subsection{Continuous-Time Formulation}
A diagonal SSM maps an input $u(t)$ to an output $y(t)$ through a state
$x(t)\in\mathbb{C}^{N}$:
\begin{equation}
\dot{x}(t) = A\,x(t) + B\,u(t), \qquad y(t) = \mathrm{Re}\!\left(\textstyle\sum_{n} C_n x_n(t)\right) + D\,u(t),
\label{eq:cont}
\end{equation}
with $A=\mathrm{diag}(A_1,\dots,A_N)$. The real part of $A_n$ sets the decay
and the imaginary part the rotation frequency; stability requires
$\mathrm{Re}(A_n)<0$.

\subsection{Discretization}
With step size $\Delta$, zero-order hold (ZOH) gives
\begin{equation}
\bar{A}_n = e^{\Delta A_n}, \qquad
\bar{B}_n = \Delta B_n \,\phi(\Delta A_n), \quad \phi(z) = \frac{e^{z}-1}{z},
\label{eq:zoh}
\end{equation}
and the recurrence $x_t = \bar{A}\,x_{t-1} + \bar{B}\,u_t$. The code evaluates
$\phi$ as a cancellation-free complex $\mathrm{expm1}(z)/z$ and switches to its
Taylor series for small $|z|$ (below $10^{-2}$ in float32, $10^{-4}$ in
float64), so value and gradient stay finite as $\Delta A\to0$; tests compare it
with a matrix-exponential reference. \mamba{}-1 and \mamba{}-2 use the
exponential-Euler rule ($\bar A=e^{\Delta A}$, input $\Delta B$); \mamba{}-3
uses the exponential-trapezoidal rule of Section~\ref{sec:m3}.

\subsection{Parallel and Recurrent Forms}
For a linear time-invariant (LTI) system the recurrence unrolls into the
causal convolution $y=K*u$ with
$K_k = \sum_n C_n \bar{B}_n \bar{A}_n^{\,k}$, $\bar{A}_n^{\,k}=e^{k\Delta A_n}$,
computed by FFT in $O(T\log T)$. At inference the recurrence advances with
$O(1)$ work per token. Selective models break time invariance and need a scan
or, for \mamba{}-2 and \mamba{}-3 here, a chunked matrix form. Every backbone in
this study implements both a parallel form (for training and for processing a
prompt) and a recurrent step (for generation), and tests require the two to
agree.

\subsection{S4D}
S4D \cite{gu2022s4d} keeps a complex diagonal transition
$A_n=-\exp(a_n)+i\pi n$ (S4D-Lin), a per-channel step size, and complex $B$ and
$C$; training uses the FFT convolution and decode a complex state of shape
$(B,D,N)$. The prefill computes the final state
$x_T=\sum_k \bar{A}^{\,T-1-k}\bar{B}u_k$ from the parallel form. A SiLU gate on a
second branch makes the block, though not the recurrence, input-dependent.

\subsection{\mamba{}-1 and \mamba{}-2}
\mamba{} \cite{gu2023mamba} makes $\Delta_t$, $B_t$ and $C_t$ functions of the
input, so $x_t = e^{\Delta_t A}x_{t-1}+\Delta_t B_t u_t$ is time-varying and
trains with a scan (here a Python loop over time). \mamba{}-2
\cite{dao2024transformers} restricts $A$ to a scalar per head, which turns the
recurrence within a chunk into the decayed, masked product
$Y=(L\odot CB^{\top})X$ with $L_{ts}=\exp(\sum_{r=s+1}^{t}\Delta_r a_h)$, plus a
small state carried between chunks (State Space Duality, SSD). The segment sums
are accumulated directly over their ranges rather than as differences of
running sums, which keeps float32 precision after a large decay. Heads read the
full $B$ and $C$ of their group.

\subsection{\mamba{}-3 and Its Chunked Form}
\label{sec:m3}
\mamba{}-3 \cite{lahoti2026mamba3} adds complex transitions
$A_n=-\exp(a_n)+i\theta_n$ and the exponential-trapezoidal rule
\begin{equation}
x_t = \alpha_t x_{t-1} + (1-\lambda_t)\Delta_t \alpha_t\, s_{t-1} + \lambda_t \Delta_t\, s_t,
\label{eq:trap}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha_t=e^{\Delta_t A}$, $s_t=B_t\otimes u_t$ and
$\lambda_t\in(0,1)$; $\lambda_t=1$ recovers exponential Euler, and
$\lambda_t=\tfrac12$ the second-order trapezoidal rule. In this implementation
$\theta$ is a static learned parameter per head and state slot (the rotation
angle $\Delta_t\theta$ depends on the input through $\Delta_t$), $B$ and $C$ are
real with readout $C^{\top}\mathrm{Re}(x_t)$, and MIMO is not implemented. A
rotation ablation fixes $\theta=0$, leaving a real decay-only transition.

\emph{Chunked form.} Writing
$L_{ts}=\prod_{r=s+1}^{t}\alpha_r=\exp(A\,D_{ts})$ with the real segment sum
$D_{ts}=\sum_{r=s+1}^{t}\Delta_r$, and using $L_{t,s+1}\alpha_{s+1}=L_{ts}$, the
complex factor inside the $\beta$ term of \eqref{eq:trap} folds into the decay:
\begin{equation}
x_t=\sum_{s\le t} L_{ts}\, s_s\, W_{ts},\quad
W_{ts}=\lambda_s\Delta_s+[s<t]\,(1-\lambda_{s+1})\Delta_{s+1}.
\label{eq:twoband}
\end{equation}
$W$ is a two-band weight matrix: $\gamma$ on the diagonal and the next step's
$\beta'$ just below it. Because $B$, $C$ and $u$ are real,
$y_t=\sum_{s\le t}G_{ts}W_{ts}u_s$ with the real
$G_{ts}=\sum_n C_{t,n}B_{s,n}\,e^{\mathrm{Re}(A_n)D_{ts}}\cos(\theta_n D_{ts})$.
This is SSD with two changes: the decay depends on the state slot, and the
input weights are two-banded. Chunks of $Q$ tokens compute $G$ from
$(Q,Q,N)$ decays built with direct masked cumsums, carry the state and the
\emph{boundary input} $B_{\mathrm{last}}\otimes u_{\mathrm{last}}$ (which the
next step's $\beta$ term still owes) into the next chunk, and run inside an
activation checkpoint per group of chunks, so backward recomputes the
$(Q,Q,N)$ tensors instead of storing them.

\subsection{Transformer Baselines}
The Transformer is a pre-norm causal Transformer with either additive
sinusoidal positions or rotary embeddings (RoPE) \cite{su2024roformer} on queries and keys. Its
attention backend is part of the variant: \texttt{auto}, or one of the SDPA
kernels FlashAttention, memory-efficient, cuDNN and math pinned with
\texttt{sdpa\_kernel}, so that a kernel that cannot run raises instead of
falling back; a written-out softmax attention is kept only as a reference.
The kernel family that actually ran is read from a profiler trace and stored
with every result. Generation uses a KV cache preallocated to the session
length; a decode step writes one position and attends one query to the cached
prefix.

\section{Implementation and Verification}
\label{sec:impl}
Every backbone exposes \texttt{prefill} (parallel form, returning the decode
state) and \texttt{step}. The test suite (pure PyTorch, CPU) checks:
prefill of $k$ tokens followed by single steps equals the full forward for
every backbone in float64 at $10^{-10}$ (relative and absolute), for $k$ from
0 to $T-1$, batch 1 and 3, several chunk boundaries and two $B/C$ groups; the
chunked \mamba{}-3 against its recurrence in float64 at $10^{-10}$ for values,
final state, boundary input and all gradients, over
$\lambda\in\{0,\tfrac12,1,\text{random}\}$, rotating and real transitions,
chunk sizes 1 to 64, tiny and underflowing decays, plus \texttt{gradcheck};
float32 against float64 at $10^{-4}$ of the output scale; RoPE against an
independent complex rotation; the attention backends against each other in
float64; naive per-head and per-slot references for the \mamba{}-2 and
\mamba{}-3 blocks; the ZOH factor against a matrix exponential; zero
\texttt{torch.compile} graph breaks in every forward; bitwise-identical
training runs for equal seeds and after resuming from a checkpoint; and that
the timed generation of the inference benchmark produces exactly the tokens of
greedy decoding by repeated full forwards. Three review passes of the model
and benchmark code found, among other things, that autograd kept every chunk's
decay tensors alive (fixed with the per-group checkpoint), that the decode
state held a view of the whole prompt (fixed), and that a per-step cache check
cost 14\% of an S4D decode step on CPU (reduced to about 4\%).

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
\emph{Tasks.} Cumulative parity (binary tokens, target the running XOR; trained
at $T=128$, tested at 128 and at 256 to 2048 for length extrapolation; chance
50\%). Selective copying (a noise stream with 3 to 5 marker-value pairs at
$T=64$; tested in distribution and out of distribution at $T=128$ with 5 to 10
pairs; chance $1/17$ per answer token). Exact copying in the formulation of
\cite{jelassi2024repeat}: reproduce a string after a separator, trained at the
single string length 32 or on lengths drawn uniformly from 1 to 32, tested at
16, 32, 64 and 128 (chance $1/26$ per letter). IMDb sentiment on 12{,}500
training reviews truncated at 512 tokens (dataset revision pinned), with 2{,}500
held-out training reviews for validation and the 25{,}000-review test split
for testing.

\emph{Runs.} Each (task, variant) is trained with three run seeds. A run seed
sets initialization, dropout and batch order; the datasets are fixed by a
separate data seed, so all runs and variants see the same examples. Training
runs with deterministic algorithms, and a run reproduces bitwise. The
per-epoch history uses the validation set; test sets are scored once after the
last epoch, with no early stopping. Tables report the mean and the sample
standard deviation over seeds.

\emph{Variants.} Per task, the Transformer keeps its original width and depth
and every SSM gets the width on its constraint grid whose total parameter
count is closest to the Transformer's (all within 10\%; counts are listed in
every table). The Transformers use the memory-efficient attention kernel in
training. The parity task adds the \mamba{}-3 rotation ablation, and
\mamba{}-1 is left out of IMDb, where its sequential scan over 512 tokens
is too slow on this GPU.

\emph{Benchmarks.} Language-model wrappers (embedding, backbone, a 32-token
head) of width 256 and two layers, equal in width rather than in parameter
count. The efficiency sweep times a training step
(batch 8) and a forward pass at $T=1$K to 32K in fp32 and under bf16 autocast;
the inference benchmark measures time to first token and per-token decode
latency after prompts of 1K to 32K tokens (batch 1, 64 new tokens, ten timed
sessions after two warm-up sessions). Timing uses CUDA events after
synchronization; tables report medians. Every point records peak allocated and
reserved memory and a spill flag: under the Windows display driver a process
that exceeds GPU memory is paged to system memory instead of failing, and such
timings are marked. A numerics benchmark compares bf16 and fp16 autocast with
float32 and float64, and a compile check counts \texttt{torch.compile} graph
breaks. All runs used the environment in Table~\ref{tab:env}.

In distribution, the Transformers and the three selective SSMs solve the

only in part.

Trained at the single length 32, the Transformers, S4D and \mamba{}-3 copy
perfectly at that length and fail at every other (at most 8.7\%), while
\mamba{}-1 and \mamba{}-2 do not learn the task even at 32 (21.8 and

on variable-length copying is the one reported by \cite{jelassi2024repeat};
the failure of the Transformers beyond the training length is a known
limitation of absolute and rotary positions at this scale.

state or the KV cache over this horizon. These are randomly initialized
models: the residual stream dominates the output (the \mamba{} blocks change
it by about 6\%), which is why the three \mamba{} variants report nearly the
same error; trained weights may behave differently. Every forward pass
compiles to one graph without breaks, and so does every decode step except
S4D's, whose parameter-version check calls \texttt{Tensor.item()} and
breaks the graph four times. \texttt{aot\_eager} reproduces eager outputs
and gradients exactly. Inductor was not measured, because Triton is not
available in this Windows environment.

\section{Discussion}
The capability results separate along the transition. A fixed convolution
(S4D) copies at a fixed offset but cannot select tokens or count; input
dependence (\mamba{}-1, \mamba{}-2) is enough for selective copying in
distribution and partly beyond it; the complex transition of \mamba{}-3
is the only one with which any run learned parity, and the $\theta=0$
ablation removes that run. Several of these effects are carried by single
seeds out of three (\mamba{}-3 on parity, the weaker \mamba{}-1 and
\mamba{}-2 seeds out of distribution), so the spread across seeds is as
informative as the means, and more seeds would be needed to rank the
selective models. Nothing in this study generalizes in length: the
Transformers, the SSMs and the rotation all fail beyond the training
lengths of parity and copying.

The systems results show the trade the architectures make. A recurrent
state of a few hundred KiB replaces a KV cache that reaches 128\,MiB at 32K
in fp32, which is what matters when many sequences share one GPU. Compute
is a different matter: without fused kernels, the selective SSMs train
more slowly than fused attention at every length measured, every SSM
prefills more slowly in bf16, and their per-token decode at batch 1 is bound
by kernel launches; only the LTI convolution of S4D keeps pace with
attention in training. The study
therefore measures the memory advantage of SSMs directly and leaves their
speed advantage to fused implementations.

\section{Limitations}
(1) Three seeds per configuration; several standard deviations are large
and the parity and out-of-distribution means rest on one or two runs.
(2) The models are small (0.1M to 3.4M parameters), the synthetic tasks
are short and IMDb uses half the training split for three epochs.
(3) The implementations are plain PyTorch; no fused SSM kernel and no
FlashAttention (not built into the Windows wheel) was measured, and
Inductor could not run without Triton. (4) S4D rather than full S4;
\mamba{}-3 without MIMO, with static rotation frequencies and real $B$ and
$C$. (5) The benchmark models have equal width rather than equal parameter
count, and \mamba{}-1 is limited to 1K in the training sweep and 8K in the
inference sweep. (6) Points marked as spilled ran under Windows paging and
their times are not GPU-resident measurements. (7) Numerics are measured at
initialization.

\section{Conclusion}
Five sequence architectures share one interface with a parallel form and
an $O(1)$ decode step, including a verified chunked form of \mamba{}-3.
Parameter-matched, three-seed experiments confirm the qualitative picture
of the SSM literature at small scale: input dependence enables selective
copying, the complex transition is what makes parity learnable here
(though unreliably), and fixed-size states fall behind attention on
variable-length copying. None of the models extrapolate in length. At 32K
tokens the SSM decode state is two to three orders of magnitude smaller
than a KV cache, while plain-PyTorch selective-SSM training and bf16
prefill remain slower than fused attention. Code, configs, the result files, the logs and the
scripts that generate every table and figure are in the accompanying
repository.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/vaibhav3000/s4-to-mamba}}
