Five sequence architectures, implemented from first principles and measured

Pure-PyTorch implementations of S4D, Mamba, Mamba-2, Mamba-3 (complex) and a Transformer baseline. Parallel and recurrent forms are proven equivalent by tests; every number below is a committed measurement, not a paper claim.

Parity tracking: 98.3% (Mamba-3) vs ≤56.3% for all real-transition baselines Selective copying: S4D collapses to 6.4%; Mamba-1/2 reach 65–67% Efficiency: 1K–32K tokens on a 6 GB RTX 4050, CUDA-event timed

Capability: where each architecture wins

Cumulative-parity tracking needs rotation; only complex, input-dependent transitions can represent it. Selective copying needs gating; LTI models cannot filter noise. Exact copying needs memory that grows with length, where attention dominates. The ranking flips with the task - that is the point.

How was this measured?

The benchmark is an experiment, not a stopwatch screenshot. Step through the actual protocol implemented in benchmarks/efficiency.py: measured methodology

  • Correctness is tested, not asserted: S4D's FFT-convolution form equals its recurrent step form; Mamba-2's chunked SSD equals its sequential reference scan; Mamba-3 reduces to the Mamba-1/2 update when λ=1 and rotation frequency is zero; decode paths equal parallel paths. Run: python -m pytest tests/ -q
  • Every figure on this page regenerates from committed JSON: results/*.json, python demo-site/build.py.
  • Theoretical O(T) and wall-clock are different claims; the python-loop scans are O(T) in operations but 10–100× slower in wall-clock - exactly why fused kernels and Mamba-2's matmul form exist.
  • Full methodology, result tables and literature reproductions: technical report (PDF) and the README.
