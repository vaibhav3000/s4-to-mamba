Five sequence architectures, implemented from first principles and measured

Pure-PyTorch implementations of S4D, Mamba, Mamba-2, Mamba-3 (complex) and a Transformer baseline. Every backbone has a parallel form for training and an O(1) decode step (a KV cache for the Transformer), and tests check the two against each other in float64. Every number below is a committed measurement, a mean over three run seeds where training is involved, not a paper claim.

Parity, five seeds: Mamba-3 72.6% mean (two seeds at 89.2 and 99.0%) vs ≤57.3% for every other model; none extrapolates to longer sequences Selective copying out of distribution: Mamba-1/2 about 50%, Mamba-3 14.4%, S4D 17.6%, Transformers 7.5 to 12.1% token accuracy Decode state after a 32K prompt: 0.14 to 0.39 MiB for the SSMs vs a 64 MiB bf16 KV cache (6 GB RTX 4050)

Capability: where each architecture wins

Cumulative parity needs the state to rotate in response to each input. Of these models only Mamba-3 has input-dependent complex transitions, and only Mamba-3 had runs that learned the task (two of five seeds); with the rotation switched off it falls back to the level of Mamba-1/2. Selective copying rewards gating: the LTI S4D fails even in distribution, and the chart shows the harder out-of-distribution test. Exact copying at the trained length of 32 is a fixed shift, which the Transformers, the LTI S4D and Mamba-3 solve and Mamba-1/2 do not.

Exact copying (in-distribution L=32)

Reproduce a 32-token string (task adapted from Jelassi et al., ICML 2024), trained at the single length 32, mean of three seeds. The answer is a fixed shift of the prompt, which the LTI S4D also learns perfectly; Mamba-1/2 do not learn it, and every model fails at lengths 64 and 128. Trained on lengths 1 to 32 instead, the Transformers lead and Mamba-3 is the best SSM (see the README).

How was this measured?

The benchmark is an experiment, not a stopwatch screenshot. Step through the actual protocol implemented in benchmarks/efficiency.py: measured methodology

  • Equivalence tests compare alternative computation paths in float64: prefill plus single decode steps against the full forward pass for every backbone, S4D's FFT convolution against its recurrence, the chunked Mamba-2 and Mamba-3 scans against their sequential forms (values and gradients, plus gradcheck), and the Mamba-2/3 blocks against naive per-head references. Run: uv run pytest -q
  • Every chart on this page regenerates from the committed results under results/: python demo-site/build.py.
  • Theoretical O(T) and wall-clock are different claims; Mamba-1's python-loop scan is O(T) in operations but takes 4.1 s per training step at 1K tokens, against 62 ms for Mamba-2's chunked matmul form, which is why fused kernels and the SSD form exist.
